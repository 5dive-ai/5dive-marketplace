# 5dive pack portraits: the one rule for every new face

Every pack's `packs/<id>/avatar.png` is the first thing a buyer sees on /marketplace. It has to sit in
the grid next to the other faces without standing out. This file describes the look the catalogue
already has, and where the catalogue was inconsistent it picks one direction and writes it down as the rule.

**Reference pack: `packs/desk/avatar.png`.** Its framing is lodar's own recrop at the locked 0.60. Put
a new face next to it, and to `perk`, which sits closest to the middle of every measured number. Nothing
else is the reference, including `maya` in OINOA's repo, which is a different brand.

**Where the house style already lives.** Our cast is generated by the creative pipeline
(`marketing/creative/cast-headshots/new-character.sh` on the 5dive host). Its `STYLE_HEAD`/`STYLE_TAIL`
prompt is the locked house style, and `tighten.py` / `check-face-ratio.py` hold the locked crop (face
0.60, roster band 0.48–0.63). This file publishes that style next to the packs, for anyone adding a face.
It adds the CI check that runs on every avatar, so the rule no longer depends on which seat made the
face. If this file and `new-character.sh` disagree, fix whichever drifted. They must say the same thing.

## Composition
- A square, photoreal head-and-shoulders studio portrait: head, neck and the top of the shoulders.
- **Tight framing.** The hair reaches, or nearly reaches, the top edge, and the shoulders are cut by
  the bottom edge. Whenever lodar corrected a face, he made it tighter, never looser ("more zoomed in to
  match our existing faces", 2026-08-10, which locked 0.60), and every re-crop since has zoomed in (#38, #43).
  Only a sliver of headroom; cropped at the upper chest. When in doubt, go tighter.
- A slight three-quarter turn toward the camera, with the eyes to the camera. The expression carries
  the character (a grin, a deadpan, a raised eyebrow), and nothing else in the frame does.
- **Solo:** one person, no stray shoulders, and no props covering the face. A hand in frame is allowed only when it is the character
  (crumb's hand on the chin, dave's fist), and never over the eyes or mouth.
- An 85mm-style portrait lens with shallow depth of field and sharp focus on the eyes: no wide-angle
  distortion and no fisheye nose.

## Face-to-frame numbers (5dive's own, measured 2026-09-29, DIVE-5188)
Measured by `scripts/check-portraits.py`: the face box is the detector's brows-to-chin box.

| metric | target | passes | what it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| `face_h` | 0.60 | 0.49 – 0.65 | face-box height ÷ image height |
| `eyes_y` | 0.42 | 0.37 – 0.47 | eye line ÷ image height, from the top |
| `centre_x` | 0.50 | 0.43 – 0.57 | face-box centre ÷ image width |

**Why not OINOA's numbers** (face 0.40, eyes 0.36, centre 0.48, all ±0.03): measured on our 30
avatars, **0 of 30 pass** them. Our median face is 0.54 (range 0.43–0.67), with the eye line at 0.42 and
the centre at 0.50. OINOA frames loose, with headroom, for a dark card. We frame tight, and lodar
approved that framing face by face. Adopting 0.40 would have loosened every face in the catalogue,
the opposite of every correction he made. Under the numbers above, **21 of 30 pass**. The other 9
are listed under Known exceptions.

**The target sits above our median (0.60 vs 0.54) on purpose.** 0.60 is lodar's locked crop
(2026-08-10, the `--face-h` default in `new-character.sh`), and the older, looser faces pull the median
down. Recent re-crops landed at the target: desk 0.60, mike 0.62 and crumb 0.58 (#43). The lower bound
agrees with the creative pipeline's own roster band (0.48–0.63, `check-face-ratio.py`, measured on the
registry 2026-09-21). The band
is wide (±0.07 to ±0.09, not OINOA's ±0.03) because this is a Haar face detector on real hair. The same
face ratio reads looser under a flat bob than under big curls, since the detector does not see hair
volume (`community/wiki/a-metric-tuned-on-the-reference-set-can-be-wrong-on-the-new-set.md`). The
band catches the face that is plainly off. It does not replace looking.

**When the number and your eye disagree, open `desk` and compare.** The tiebreak is a face that is
already approved, not the number and not a fresh opinion.

## Background and light
- **A clean, seamless studio backdrop in solid light grey, #E7E7E9, with no texture, gradient or
  vignette.** That is the locked prompt. Desk, perk, dub and reps show it: their corners measure #DFDEE0
  to #E8E8EA.
- **Rule where the set is inconsistent:** the generator drifts from the lock. 11 of our 30 came out a
  cooler blue-grey (#BAC1C9 – #CCD3DC: ada, anton, bo, don, goodwin, mike, quant, root, theo, vesper,
  ward), and two a darker grey (axe #AEB2B6, ace #C5C5C8). The lock stands, and it is also the majority
  (17 of 30 read as light neutral grey). A new face whose corners come out visibly blue or dark is
  re-rolled, not shipped. The existing ones stay until they are re-rendered for another reason.
- Soft, even, frontal light from a large front softbox, with gentle natural shadows and a neutral colour
  grade. Photoreal, with natural skin texture and no beautifying. No rim light, no coloured light
  and no dramatic side-lighting.
- No frame, ring, border, vignette or badge. The photo runs edge to edge.

## Wardrobe
- **The character's own clothes, chosen for the role.** This is 5dive's differentiator, and it is not a
  uniform: a suit for goodwin and don, a hoodie for reps and root, a chef's jacket and towel for mise,
  a lanyard for clicker, headphones for lilbro.
- Plain fabrics in muted colours (navy, charcoal, black, white, denim, olive, one warm accent at most).
- No readable text, logos or brand marks on the clothes.

## Never
- No text, logos, watermarks, emoji or UI in the image.
- No 5dive, OINOA or model-vendor branding.
- No real people and no lookalikes of public figures. Our characters are AI-generated brand characters.
- No illustration, 3D or cartoon style: the catalogue is photoreal, and one drawn face breaks the grid.
- No OINOA house style: no graphite background, gold rim light, constellation dots, ivory blazer or pin.

## Files
- **Source:** render at **1000x1000 or larger**, and keep the wide original (the creative pipeline keeps
  it as `out/<id>-wide.png`). Every re-crop comes from the wide, never a re-roll: a re-roll draws a new face.
- **`avatar.png`:** a square PNG, **at least 512px**. The creative pipeline ships 1254x1254 (16 packs);
  `scripts/frame-portrait.py` here writes 512x512 (14 packs are 512). Both are fine, and the check
  enforces square and at least 512.
- **Workflow without the creative pipeline** (a contributor, or a face made elsewhere):
  ```bash
  python3 -m venv /tmp/cv && /tmp/cv/bin/pip install "opencv-python-headless==4.14.0.94"
  /tmp/cv/bin/python scripts/frame-portrait.py source.png packs/<id>/avatar.png   # crop to the targets, 512px
  /tmp/cv/bin/python scripts/check-portraits.py packs/<id>/avatar.png             # must print ok
  ```
  Then look at it next to `desk`. Re-render instead of cropping when the crop cannot reach the target:
  the script warns when the crop is under 512px (the face is too small in the source), or the head sits
  on the top edge with no room to move it.
- CI (`.github/workflows/checks.yml`, the `portraits` job) runs the check on every `packs/*/avatar.png`.

## Prompt template
The locked house style is `STYLE_HEAD` + `STYLE_TAIL` from `new-character.sh`, quoted verbatim, followed by
the subject line. Only `{SUBJECT}` changes per character:

> Square 1:1 corporate executive headshot, matching a locked house style. Clean seamless studio
> backdrop, solid cool light-grey #E7E7E9, no texture or vignette. Soft even frontal lighting from a
> large front softbox, gentle natural shadows, polished professional look, neutral color grade. Framing:
> TIGHT head-and-shoulders portrait — the head fills most of the frame, top of the hair close to the top
> edge, cropped at the upper chest, only a sliver of headroom. Slight three-quarter turn toward camera,
> eyes to camera. Do NOT pull back to a wide chest-up or half-body shot. SOLO subject only — absolutely
> no other people or stray shoulders in frame. Photoreal, sharp focus, shallow depth of field, natural
> skin texture, no beautifying.
>
> Subject: {NAME}, {age}, {one-line look and role}. {Expression that carries the character}. {Hair}.
> Wearing {role-appropriate clothes, plain muted colours, no logos or readable text}. No text, logos or
> watermarks in the image.

"Corporate executive headshot" names the *photographic* style (studio, softbox, framing), not the
wardrobe. The subject line's role clothes win, as the Wardrobe section says. Attach
`packs/desk/avatar.png` as the style reference when inventing a new face. Pass `--ref` in
`new-character.sh` only to match an existing person.

reframed here: lodar judges framing per face. The check reports these as `known` rather than failing,
and **fails any entry that starts passing**, so remove a line when its face is fixed. Do not add a new
pack here: a new face meets the band.

- anton: face_h 0.43, eyes_y 0.34, loose (long hair). Its 1254px source allows a crop to the target.

- mise: face_h 0.67, tight (bald head, approved as the house look in DIVE-3358). Keep.
- marcus: centre_x 0.65, the face sits right of centre. It is 512px, so a fix is a re-render.
- ace: centre_x 0.42, slightly left. It is 512px (DIVE-4989 recast).
- axe: eyes_y 0.51, the face sits low. It is 512px (DIVE-4989 recast).
- dario: eyes_y 0.48, a tilted head. It is 512px.
