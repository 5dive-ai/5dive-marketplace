# Follow-up ladder

Most things are not refused, they are forgotten. The job is to be the thing that does not
forget, on a schedule, in a register that stays professional at rung five, and to stop the
moment it lands.

## The one rule that governs everything

**The ladder is a schedule, not a mood.** Each rung is decided in advance — when it sends,
what it says, which channel it uses, and what it escalates to. Nothing about the sequence
depends on how the chaser feels on the day, which is the only reason it can run to rung six
without becoming either apologetic or aggressive. The two failure modes are the same
failure: improvising the next message.

Corollary: **never apologise for following up.** "Sorry to bother you again" concedes that
asking was an imposition, which invites another non-reply. You are not bothering anyone;
they owe a thing and you are naming it.

## Procedure

1. **Write down what is owed, by whom, and since when.** If any of those three is unclear,
   the first message establishes it rather than chasing it. You cannot escalate a thing you
   have not stated.
2. **Confirm it was actually asked.** A remarkable share of "they never replied" is a thing
   nobody sent, sent to the wrong address, or asked so obliquely that no request was made.
   Check before rung one.
3. **Pick the ladder** from `references/ladder-cadences.md` — invoices, a stalled reply and
   a verbal yes have different rungs and different legitimate endings.
4. **Send rung one on schedule** and log it. Every rung is logged with date, channel and
   what was said; the log is what lets rung five reference the whole history in one line.
5. **Change exactly one variable per rung:** tone, channel, or who is addressed. Changing
   all three at once reads as panic and skips the rungs that usually work.
6. **Escalate the ADDRESSEE, not the volume.** Rung four goes to their manager, their AP
   department, or the person who wanted the thing — not to the same inbox in bold.
7. **Stop the day it lands.** Acknowledge in one line, close the loop, and remove it from
   the ladder immediately. A chase that continues after payment is the only version of this
   that damages the relationship.
8. **Report what the ladder learned.** Which rung usually works, who never answers rung
   one, and whether a class of item (a payment term, an approval step) is structurally
   slow. That is a process finding, not a chasing problem.

## Message shape, every rung

- **One ask, at the top.** The amount, the question, or the document, in the first line.
- **The history in one clause, factually.** "Invoiced 14 Aug, due 28 Aug, unpaid as of
  today." No adjectives, no scorekeeping.
- **A specific next action with a date.** "Can you confirm by Thursday" beats "please
  advise at your earliest convenience", which asks for nothing and gets it.
- **An easy exit.** "If this is no longer going ahead, tell me and I will close it." A
  cheap no is worth more than an expensive silence, and it ends the ladder honestly.
- **No guilt, no threat, no sarcasm, no passive aggression.** Not at rung two, not at rung
  six. The register is the asset.

## Hard rules

- **Never invent a consequence you cannot impose.** No fake final notices, no invented late
  fees, no implied legal action. State only what will actually happen, and only if it will.
- **Never state a contractual term, interest rate, or penalty you have not read.** Quote the
  terms or say nothing.
- **Never contact anyone outside the agreed channels** — no personal phone, no home address,
  no showing up, no messaging their colleagues about the debt, no public callouts. Chasing
  in public is a reputational act, not a collection method, and in many places it is
  regulated conduct.
- **Stop immediately on a written dispute, a hardship statement, a bereavement, or any
  mention of legal representation.** Route to a human, same day, and pause the ladder.
- **Never contact a third party about someone's debt** unless they are the designated AP or
  billing contact.
- **Never resume a stopped ladder without a human decision**, and never restart the rungs
  from one to make the count look fresh.
- **Log every rung.** An unlogged chase is why people get chased twice for one thing.

## Worked example

An invoice, £4,200, 30-day terms, on the invoice ladder. Log as it ran:

```
D0   invoiced. terms net-30.
D31  RUNG 1 — email, AP contact, neutral:
     "Invoice 1042, £4,200, was due yesterday (30 Aug). Can you confirm it is scheduled?
      If it is already paid, ignore this and I will match it up."
D38  RUNG 2 — email, same contact, reply-in-thread, one variable changed (directness):
     "Following up on invoice 1042, £4,200, now 8 days past due. Is there anything you
      need from me to release it — a PO number, a resend, a different address?"
D45  RUNG 3 — email + phone, same contact. Voicemail left, logged.
     "Invoice 1042, £4,200, 15 days past due, no response to two emails. Calling today.
      If this is stuck internally, tell me where and I will chase the right person."
D52  RUNG 4 — ADDRESSEE CHANGES: the project sponsor who commissioned the work, CC AP.
     "Invoice 1042 for the work delivered in July is 22 days past due and I have not been
      able to reach AP (emails 31/38 Aug, call 14 Sep, all logged). Can you point me at
      whoever can release it? Happy to be told it is queued."
D53  landed. paid in full.
D53  STOP. One line: "Received, thank you — closing this out. Nothing further needed."
     Removed from ladder same day.
```

What made it work, and what it deliberately never did:

- **Rung 4 changed who, not how loud.** The sponsor wanted the work; AP does not. Every
  rung before it stayed in one register.
- **Every rung offered an easy exit** ("if it is already paid, ignore this", "tell me where
  it is stuck"). Two of the four invited a cheap answer that would have ended the ladder.
- **No consequence was ever invented.** No final notice, no interest, no legal language —
  the terms did not provide for them, so they were not mentioned.
- **The history was factual at every rung.** "22 days past due, emails on these dates, all
  logged" is not scorekeeping, it is the reason the escalation to the sponsor reads as
  reasonable rather than as complaining.
- **It stopped the same day.** A rung-five draft existed and was discarded unsent.

Contrast, the same D52 message written from a mood rather than a ladder:

```
"This is now the FOURTH time I've had to chase this. I'd have thought a company of your
size could pay an invoice on time. Please treat this as a final notice."
```

Three errors in three lines: it scorekeeps, it insults the payer you still need, and
"final notice" is a consequence the terms do not support — so it is either empty or it is a
claim you cannot back. It also makes the sponsor, whose help you are asking for, the
audience for a complaint about their own company.

## Handoff

Return: the item, what is owed and since when, which ladder and which rung is next with its
date, the drafted message for that rung, and the log so far. Nothing sends without a yes.
For a batch, return the items ordered by rung reached (highest first), and the process
finding if one class of item is structurally slow.

See `references/ladder-cadences.md` for the rungs per item type and the legitimate endings.
