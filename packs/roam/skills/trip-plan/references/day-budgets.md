# Day budgets, sequencing rules and the book-now table

Lookup tables for `trip-plan`. Everything here is a PLANNING assumption, not a live fact.
Prices, hours and entry rules are always confirmed at the source before booking.

## How much a day actually holds

| Day type | Anchors it holds | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival day, short hop (under ~4h, no time change) | 1 | Plus a meal. Nothing ticketed. |
| Arrival day, long haul eastbound | 0 | A walk and an early dinner. Protect the next morning. |
| Arrival day, long haul westbound | 1 | You will wake at 04:00 anyway. Use the early morning on day 2. |
| Full day, one neighbourhood, on foot | 3 | The realistic maximum for most travellers. |
| Full day with a train each way (day trip) | 1, maybe 2 | The travel is one of the anchors, whether you count it or not. |
| Move day between bases | 1 | Half a day at each end. Luggage decides everything. |
| Rain day | 2 | Indoor things absorb more time and less energy. |
| Departure day | 0 | Breakfast and the station. |
| Day with a child under 5, or a mobility limit | 2 | And one of them is near the base. |

## Transit padding

| Leg | Pad on top of the timetable |
|---|---|
| First journey in an unfamiliar city | Double your estimate |
| Metro or subway, same city | 15 min each end (walk, ticket, wrong exit) |
| Intercity train, seat reserved | 30 min before, 20 min after |
| Bus, anywhere | 30 min, and a fallback |
| Airport to city, unfamiliar | 90 min of attention even if the ride is 40 |
| Ferry or a mountain lift | Check the last departure before planning the day. That time, not sunset, ends the day |

## Book now vs book later

| Book now | Why |
|---|---|
| Long-haul flights and any high-season flight | Price only moves one way |
| First night's accommodation, always | You do not want a decision after a flight |
| All nights in high season or during a local festival | Whole cities sell out |
| Timed-entry attractions | The slot shapes the day, so it must exist first |
| Capacity-capped things: permits, ferries, tiny restaurants, guided-only sites | Cannot be recovered later |
| The last leg home (seat, transfer) | The one failure with a real cost |

| Book later | Why |
|---|---|
| Most restaurants | One dinner a day is enough structure |
| Day trips | Weather and energy decide these on the day |
| Anything outdoors | Needs the swap, not the reservation |
| Intra-city transport cards | Bought on arrival, usually cheaper |

## Sequencing rules that repeat

1. Crowded outdoor icon: first thing in the morning or last light, never midday.
2. Museums: check the weekly closing day BEFORE assigning areas to days.
3. Markets: mornings. Many are dead or shut by early afternoon.
4. Religious sites: check service times and dress requirements; both can void a slot.
5. Viewpoints: pick either sunrise or sunset for the trip, not both, or nobody sleeps.
6. Put the physically hardest day second or third, never first and never last.
7. One booked dinner per day, near where the day already ends.
8. Every outdoor anchor is written with its indoor swap on the same line.

## The four boring unlocks, per trip

- **Money**: does this place still want cash, and for what.
- **Data**: eSIM, roaming, or offline maps downloaded before landing.
- **Transport**: the card or app locals use, and where you get it.
- **Power**: plug type and whether the group has one adapter or four.

## Group constraints to ask about before choosing a base

Nap schedule, knees or stairs, heights, dietary or fasting needs, curfews, motion sickness,
who is paying and how they want to split it, and how early anyone is willing to get up.
The answer to the last one silently sets what the trip can include.
