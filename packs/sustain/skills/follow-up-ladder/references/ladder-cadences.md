# Ladder cadences

Rungs, intervals, and legitimate endings per item type. Intervals are from the previous
rung, not from day zero. Every ladder has an ENDING — a chase with no defined end is not a
ladder, it is harassment with a spreadsheet.

Local law and contract terms override everything here. Where a jurisdiction regulates debt
collection conduct or timing, that wins and a human decides.

## Unpaid invoice (a debt, and the most regulated of these)

| rung | when | channel | change | register |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | due date +1 | email, AP contact | — | neutral, assume an oversight |
| 2 | +7 | email, same thread | directness | ask what is blocking release |
| 3 | +7 | email + one call | channel | offer to chase internally for them |
| 4 | +7 | email to sponsor, CC AP | **addressee** | factual history, ask for a route |
| 5 | +7 | email, account owner | addressee | state what happens next, only if true |
| 6 | +14 | human decision | — | write-off, payment plan, or escalation |

Ending: paid, a written payment plan, a formal dispute (STOP, route to a human), or a
decision to write it off. Never an indefinite loop.

Stop conditions that override the schedule: written dispute, hardship, bereavement, any
mention of legal representation or insolvency. Pause, log, hand to a human the same day.

## Unanswered email (no money, a request)

| rung | when | channel | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | +3 working days | reply in thread | — |
| 2 | +5 | new email, better subject line | framing — the original may simply not have read as a request |
| 3 | +7 | different channel (chat, phone) | channel |
| 4 | +7 | ask a colleague if this is the right person | **addressee** |

Ending: an answer, a redirect, or **three rungs of silence read as a no**. Say so out loud
and close it: "I have not heard back, so I am assuming this is not going ahead — tell me if
that is wrong." That sentence recovers a surprising number of them, and when it does not it
ends the item honestly instead of leaving it open forever.

## Verbal yes, never confirmed

The fastest ladder, because the risk is ambiguity rather than avoidance.

| rung | when | channel | purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | same day | email | write down what was agreed, ask them to confirm or correct |
| 2 | +2 | reply in thread | "assuming this is right unless I hear otherwise, starting Monday" |
| 3 | +3 | their manager or the doc | make the assumption visible where the work is tracked |

Ending: written confirmation, a correction (equally good — it surfaced a mismatch), or a
recorded assumption everyone can see. Do not begin irreversible or expensive work on rung
2's assumption; make it visible first.

## Approval or signature owed

| rung | when | channel | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | +2 working days | email, approver | — |
| 2 | +3 | email + name the cost of delay, factually | stakes ("this slips the 14th") |
| 3 | +3 | approver's manager or the delegate | **addressee** |
| 4 | +5 | decision: proceed without it, or the thing does not happen | — |

Ending: signed, delegated, or an explicit decision to proceed unsigned — which is a
human's call and must be recorded as one, never assumed by silence.

## Cross-cutting rules

- **One variable per rung.** Tone, channel, or addressee. Never two.
- **Escalation means a new addressee**, not a louder message to the same one.
- **Every rung is logged** with date, channel, and what was said. Rung four's credibility
  is entirely built from that log.
- **Every rung offers an exit.** A cheap no ends the ladder honourably and it is a better
  outcome than a slow yes.
- **Stop the day it lands.** Acknowledge, close, remove. Same day.
- **Never restart a ladder at rung one** to reset the optics. The history is the point.
